About me

My career has followed one thread: getting closer to where data creates real value. I started in research, moved into quality engineering, where I spent years making sure data and software could be trusted by customers, and then into building machine learning systems myself.

What I've learned along the way: the best technical solution only matters if people can act on it. Whether I'm tracing a problem to its root cause, explaining findings to non-technical stakeholders or simplifying something until a team can use it at a glance, I care about the step from "technically correct" to "actually useful."

What excites me most is AI moving from the screen into the real world: robotics, simulation and industrial AI, where data comes from machines and sensors and the hardest problems are the rare failures no one sees coming.

I learn by building. For me, the fastest way to understand a new technology is to make something with it, and I enjoy sharing what I learn with the people around me.

Anomaly detection in manufacturing data

A slow fault in a test bench shifted calibration a little at a time. Every single reading looked normal, and there were no labelled failures to learn from.

I built an unsupervised system on Azure Databricks that learns what normal looks like, combines machine learning with the quality metric engineers already trust, and sends the quality team a simple weekly verdict.

Built with: PySpark, Azure Databricks, Delta Lake, MLflow, Isolation Forest, K-Means, CPK
